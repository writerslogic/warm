# Future card proposal: H-C-3 — exhaustive raw-history traversal

1. Limitation: the audited artifacts preserve useful positive witnesses but lack an exhaustive semantic coverage and retained-prompt account; selected-session completeness was previously overstated as source completeness.
2. Mechanism: traverse every physical raw-source occurrence under one fixed partition policy, retain occurrence/date/role identities, and let query-aware readers inspect bounded partitions plus raw counterevidence; report unvisited or undecided partitions explicitly. No extracted event ledger is assumed complete.
3. Assumption challenged: top-k retrieval plus a finite set of citations suffices for exact aggregates and abstentions. Physical traversal coverage is an accounting fact, never by itself semantic absence or entailment.
4. Prior art: [Recursive Language Models v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24601v3) already decomposes external context into inspected snippets and recursive calls. A proposed contribution is deterministic coverage/cost accounting tied to retained raw occurrences and unresolved evidence states; novelty is unproved. [TReMu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01630v2) is the separate temporal-operator family; [the representation-bottleneck preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04243v1) motivates testing extraction rather than assuming it succeeds.
5. Existing WARM to reuse: `longmemeval_verifier_sources.py:42-184` already unions cited premises and counterevidence under a cap; `longmemeval_answer_evidence.py:166-215` retains source identities and records cap drops. Merely adding citations, a verifier prompt or raw-source retention duplicates existing behavior. The inspected historical runner selects top-k chunks; no exhaustive traversal guarantee was found in these paths. This is a bounded inspection, not a repository-wide novelty claim.
6. Primitive model: for raw history H tokens, partition capacity b, per-call overhead h and returned evidence e, one complete pass costs at least H + ceil(H/b)*h input tokens and at least ceil(H/b) calls before reconciliation; final context and repeated raw counterevidence add cost. Source hashing or prefix reuse can change storage/billing, not prove semantic coverage. No measured H, speedup or affordable operating point is claimed.
7. Frontier prediction: under matched reader/judge/resource boundaries, complete traversal and explicit unresolved-evidence states could eliminate at least half of genuine retrieval/coverage failures; this is a new unmeasured hypothesis, not a new denominator or threshold for H-C-2. Source contradictions still require instrument adjudication.
8. Smallest decisive action: a separately registered offline cost/coverage accounting pass over already exposed artifacts, using physical occurrence identities and the actual declared reader/cost constraints. Compare full traversal against existing capped packets before any reader work; no fresh per-item holdout look and no paid development.
9. Acceptance for that necessary gate: all source occurrences and duplicate dates are preserved without identity collisions; every omitted or undecided partition is explicit; the full-pass lower bound fits the independently declared row context/cost boundary. Reject on any coverage loss or a cost lower bound that exceeds that boundary; an unspecified boundary cannot be treated as a pass. Semantic gain remains unproved until a later separately frozen transfer gate with an independent oracle.
10. Risks and bias: a full pass can be too costly, semantic filtering can still omit evidence, cross-partition identity can fail, and recursive outputs can introduce unsupported facts. Prefer raw spans over unsupported summaries; do not equate visitation with understanding. This deliberately tests the alternative that threatens the value of selective retrieval. H-C-2 remains rejected regardless of the successor outcome.
